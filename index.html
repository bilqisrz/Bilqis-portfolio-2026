Won · $255K TCV · 3-Year

Checkatrade — Voice of Customer Transformation

Competitive rip-out against an incumbent CX provider. Sold into a new CEO's mandate to digitise the £59B UK home services sector — starting with a $30K CJM engagement, then a $255K platform deal, all in four months.

30%→15%

Discovery — Checkatrade had 50,000 members churning at 30% (£20M/yr lost) with no clear visibility into why. Two SMS NPS touchpoints and basic reporting. No one owned the insight.

Strategy — Rather than pitching the platform, I ran a free in-person workshop that mapped their end-to-end member journey and surfaced three critical gaps: fragmented data, no self-serve, and no early-warning signal for churn risk. This positioned me as a trusted advisor — not a vendor.

Win — Sold a $30K CJM remapping engagement before any platform discussion. Co-created a one-page business case with the Head of CX (champion) in the language the COO would buy. When an RFP dropped mid-cycle, we already had executive alignment and a pilot running — which put us ahead of Qualtrics and Medallia from day one.

Results — Won $255K TCV over three years. Response rates increased 3% in the first cycle. Delivery team received 10/10 CSAT. Within six months, the scope expanded to include digital feedback ahead of their app launch.

Greencore — Unsolicited RFP, Zero Lead Time

Two days to respond to an unsolicited RFP from the UK's largest food-to-go manufacturer (supplying M&S, Waitrose, Sainsbury's). No existing solution. No pricing structure. Internal resistance. Won anyway.

6-fig

The challenge — An unsolicited RFP arrived with 48 hours to respond. We had no product built for their exact requirements, no agreed pricing, and a senior leadership team sceptical about committing resources on a long-shot.

How I got internal buy-in — I framed the opportunity not as revenue risk but as an intelligence exercise: a chance to deeply understand enterprise food manufacturing at scale. That reframe unlocked leadership support and the cross-functional resources needed to submit.

The proposal — Spent months (post-submission) studying the market alongside marketing and product. Navigated multiple stakeholders through solution reviews, custom pricing negotiations, and iterations. Used MEDDPICC throughout and co-created business cases with internal champions to align Greencore's executives with ours.

Result — Won a 3-year, six-figure contract with significant upsell potential across Greencore's supplier network. The deal became the blueprint for pursuing high-value accounts in retail and food manufacturing.

First Public Sector Win · £250K TCV

Borough Council — First Public Sector Logo

Net-new, self-sourced deal. A council fielding 30–40K calls per quarter at £1.67/call. I anchored the entire commercial case in cost-of-inaction — not product features — and closed a 3-year contract in a notoriously slow buying environment.

£250K
TCV (3-year)
30%
Call volume deflection
£75K+
Annual contact centre savings

The approach — Rather than leading with platform capability, I built a cost-per-contact model using the council's own operational data. £1.67 per call × 35,000 calls/quarter = £240K+ annual contact centre spend with no digital fallback.

Stakeholder management — Mapped and engaged across a buying committee spanning CX, digital transformation, and finance. Tailored every message: ROI for finance, resident outcomes for CX, risk reduction for digital. Ran a structured discovery to validate a 30% call deflection target.

Unlock — Reframing the conversation away from features toward cost-of-inaction. Once the £1.67 figure was on the table alongside a 30% deflection projection, the ROI logic became self-evident and the deal essentially wrote its own approval path.

Legacy — The discovery framework, cost-per-contact model, and transformation narrative I built became reusable assets for subsequent public sector conversations — opening a new vertical playbook.

I grew up between cultures — which gave me an early instinct for reading people, adapting quickly, and building trust with strangers. Before SaaS, I coordinated multinational cabin crew teams. It turns out the skills transfer perfectly: stay calm under pressure, listen more than you talk, and make everyone feel they're the most important person in the room.

I moved into tech sales because I love the intellectual puzzle of enterprise deals. Not the transactional kind — the kind where you have to understand a business deeply enough to show them something they couldn't see themselves. I use MEDDPICC, Challenger, and value-based selling not as scripts but as lenses.

My superpower is the one-page business case. I learned it from Nate Nasralla and it changed everything. When you co-create a business case with your champion, you stop being a vendor and start being a partner. You write in their language. You prepare them to sell internally. And you build the urgency that accelerates decisions.

I'm currently targeting the compliance and security space — a £12.8B market with high stakes, genuine purpose (fighting financial crime, protecting economies), and the kind of complex, consultative sales motion I thrive in.

Beyond work — I care deeply about people, and I think that shows in how I sell. I'm drawn to roles and companies where the solution genuinely makes someone's working life better. Outside the office, I'm curious about culture, travel (lived across three continents), and anything that involves understanding how people make decisions.
